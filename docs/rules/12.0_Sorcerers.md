# Sorcerer Documentation

## [12.0] Sorcerers

GENERAL RULE:

A Sorcerer is a human (**non**-magical) figure who has become skilled at a type or types of magic. In the game this is represented by a Sorcerer being of a certain specific color or combination of colors. There are five basic types of Sorcerers. These are:

1. **One-color:** controls only one of the colors of magic. One-color Sorcerers are either Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red or Purple.
2. **Two-color:** controls two of the colors of magic. Two-color Sorcerers are any of the fifteen possible combinations of two of the six colors (e.g., Blue-Orange).
3. **Three-Color:** controls three of the colors of magic. Three-color Sorcerers are any of the twenty three-color combinations of the six colors (e.g., Blue-Yellow-Red).
4. **White:** controls all of the colors of magic, including White.
5. **Black:** controls no represented magical color, but rather acts as an intelligent vortex. The Black Sorcerer is never affected by vortexes. 

A Sorcerer of two or more colors has equal power over the colors that he controls. In other words, a Green-Red Sorcerer functions in the same manner as both a Green Sorcerer and a Red Sorcerer.

#### [12.1] EFFECT ON COMBAT
**[12.11]** A Sorcerer may only use his full (printed) Combat Strength when stacked with Friendly (non-Sorcerer) units which match one of the Sorcerer&apos;s colors. No more than one Sorcerer may add his Combat Strength to a given stack of Friendly units. The Combat Strength added to the stack may never exceed the total Combat Strength of the **other** Friendly units in the hex.

**Example:** A Purple-Green-Orange Sorcerer (with a Combat Strength of 6) stacked with a Purple demonic infantry unit (with a Combat Strength of 5) would have a combined Combat Strength of 10 (5+5). The amount of Combat Strength Points that a Sorcerer adds to the stack of Friendly units is determined before any additions to the Combat Strength are made as a result of Color Augmentation. The Color Augmentation is dictated by the coloro of the magical  unit (Purple in the example) **not** by any of the other colors that the Sorcerer may control (Green or Orange in the example). 

**[12.12]** When alone in a hex, a Sorcerer&apos;s Combat Strength is reduced to half the printed value (reduced downward). Note that this does not affect the power of the Sorcerer&apos;s Magic Bolts -- they remain a the level of the printed strength.

**[12.13]** When in a Gray hex (regardless of the type of units stacked with him), a Sorcerer&apos;s value is reduced to half the printed value.

**[12.14]** When stacked with only human units, a Sorcerer&apos;s strength is reduced to **one-half** the printed value (maximum). The amount of strength a Sorcerer may contribute to such a stack is either equal to the Combat Strength of the human units, or one-half the Sorcerer&apos;s printed Combat Strength, whichever is **less**. Example: a three-color, full strength Sorcerer is stacked with two depleted human infantry units. The total Combat Strength in the hex is **four** (two Strength Points of human infantry, plus two out of the three of the Sorcerer's halved Combat Strength). 

**[12.15]** Be aware that the only situation in which a stack gets the benefit of Color Augmentation is when that stack contains a **magical** unit or units (i.e., an air dragon, demonic infantry, troll unit or a fortress). Simply because a stack contains a Sorcerer, this does not mean that a stack will benefit from Color Augmentation - a Sorcerer is **not** a magical unit.

**[12.16]** If a stack contains a Friendly magical unit of a color in which a Sorcerer does **not** have power, the Sorcerer's effective value is halved (but the stack nevertheless experiences the benefit of Color Augmentation based on the color of the magical unit).

**[12.17]** A Sorcerer can **never** be reduced by more than one step in a given Phase. No matter what the combat result, no matter how many times the Sorcerer may be attacked in a Phase (by a series of Magic Bolts or by more than one Player in a multi-Player game), **a given Sorcerer in a given hex can never suffer more than a one-step loss in a given
Phase**. Therefore, the only way in which Sorcerers can be eliminated is to successfully attack them in two successive Phases, reducing them by one step in each Phase, before they have time to recover from depletion.

#### [12.2] PERFORMANCE OF CONJURATION AND MAGICAL TASKS
Sorcerers are the **only** units which may conjure new magical units and perform other magical tasks during the Movement and Conjuration Phase ([see Case 6.0](6.0_Movement.md)). Players should note that the ability to perform magical tasks is based solely on the expenditure of the Sorcerer&apos;s **Movement Allowance**. A Sorcerer&apos;s **Combat Strength** has no effect on the ability to perform magical tasks.

#### [12.3] ATTRITION INSULATION
A full strength Sorcerer and a single same-color magical unit stacked with him, **never** undergo attrition. If there are **two** magical units  in the hex, and/or if the Sorcerer is depleted and/or does not have power in the color of the units -- then the magical units are vulnerable to attrition. 

> _Editors note: The original player&apos;s manual uses [12.41] and [12.42] here. However, this is a typo. They should have used [12.31] and [12.32]. Corrected in this version._

**[12.31]** Sorcerers may undeplete Friendly units during the Movement and Conjuration Phase ([see Case 6.52](6.0_Movement.md#652)).

**[12.32]** Sorcerers themselves may be undepleted only through the expenditure of their **own** Movement Points. A Sorcerer may never undeplete another Sorcerer. When Sorcerers become undepleted, this act occurs at the **end** of the Movement and Conjuration Phase.
