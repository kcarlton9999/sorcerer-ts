# Sorcerer Documentation

## [19.0] MULTI-PLAYER SCENARIOS

The following Scenarios are designed to be played by three to six Players.

#### [18.1] THE BROKEN TRIUMVERATE
After the War for Dominance, a somewhat stable period ensured, during which a triumverate (who unimaginatively called themselves "The Three”) reigned. The Graylands were divided amongst them, the White Realms allowed to revert, and a new Academy built with the Examiners consisting of The Three. As with all egotistical humans, The Three eventually had a falling-out over trivial matters. Cire attempted to pass his wife’s inept cousin into the Second Circle of Apprenticeship by feeding him answers and surreptitiously helping him on his spells. Lume, meanwhile, was skimming off about 10% of all the tuition money. Gorm had fallen in love with a she-demon and had given her a child. All of these indiscretions and lapses came to light during a stormy session of the Examiners (wherein the unfortunate cousin was attempting to pass and Lume caught him out). Accusations and recriminations brought the pot to a boil. The Three each retired to their respective fortresses, and each determined to be The One.

**[19.11]** STARTING FORCES
**Blue-Orange-Player* (Cire):
One Blue-Orange Sorcerer (Cire)
One Blue Sorcerer
One Orange Sorcerer
Seven human infantry
One Blue (4) fortress within three hexes of Ivor-Vorranna (1016)
One Orange (4) fortress within three hexes of Cessa-Abirran (2017)

**Green-Red Player** (Lume):   
One Green-Red Sorcerer (Lume)
One Green Sorcerer
One Red Sorcerer
Seven human infantry
One Green (4) fortress within three hexes of Selvass (1905)
One Red (4) fortress within three hexes of Lyann-Dritta (1205)
  
**Yellow-Purple Player** (Gorm):
One Yellow-Purple Sorcerer (Gorm)
One Yellow Sorcerer
One Purple Sorcerer
Seven human infantry
One Yellow (4) fortress within three hexes of Amma-Avattar (0711)
One Purple (4) fortress within three hexes of Riddella-Bes (1710)

GENERAL DEPLOYMENT:   
Sorcerers in fortresses; human infantry within three hexes of fortresses. Alternatively, Players may deploy as described in 16.0.

**[19.12]** VICTORY CONDITIONS   
As per 15.0. Player with the second highest Victory Points total should be considered second-place winner. Victory should be judged at the end of **eight** Game-Turns.

#### [19.2] THE TASK OF THE STONES OF POWER

When Ussa grew wise in years (and not too right in the head), he foresaw his own death. The runes told him it would be connected somehow with cooking meat. He resolved to pass along his powers to one of his three sons ‘ere he depart and some upstart two-power Sorcerer usurp one of the colors of his line. Since he loved (and mistreated) ail his sons equally, he set them a task to decide who would be his prime beneficiary. He called his sons to his townhall in Amma-Avattar, and to them he said: "In nine cities have I placed three Stones of Power each. And upon each Stone have I set a Seal of chaos. The first of my sons who shall gather up six Stones of his color and set them in a field of power beyond this city gate shall have set upon him a robe of three colors, the ring of three colors, the book of three colors... robe, ring and book. And I shall pass from this vale.”
Opa. Acu and Dorn crashed into each other as they beelined to the door of the townhall. Now their father had enjoined them from killing each other (so they couldn’t just simply stab each other in the alleyway). But they had not become Sorcerers by shirking their duty to practice deviousness. As each to a separate city sped, he set upon the other’s path demons, brigands, magic bolts and whatever. As Stones were gathered by one, they would be stolen by another. Acu actually managed to get himself killed by one of the Seals; Dorn was severely beaten by some freelance thugs in Lyann-Dritta; Opa lost his Color Sword in a
gambling house. As time sifted by, Ussa grew impatient with his sons, and in a peevish manner of old men bestowed his charms of power on his very good old friend, Shamma, the RiverWitch (who for so long had bestowed her charms on him). Dorn and Opa returned to find a three-color Sorceress in mourning for their capricious father (who had died of food poisoning after eating a meal of raw chopped meat).

**[19.21]** STARTING FORCES   
**Blue Player** (Acu)   
One Blue Sorcerer (Acu)  
Five human infantry  

**Yellow Player** (Dorn)  
One Yellow Sorcerer (Dorn)  
Five human infantry  

**Red Player** (Opa)  
One Red Sorcerer (Opa)  
Five human infantry   

GENERAL DEPLOYMENT:   
Each Sorcerer and as many as two of his human infantry deploy in a hex of his color within two hexes of Amma-Avattar. Each Player may deploy the remainder of his human infantry in two or more of the nine other Gray cities. These units may be deployed secretly or openly.

**[19.22]** VICTORY CONDITIONS   
The first Player to accumulate six Stones of Power of his color in his starting hex, wins. The Stones must be under the Player’s undisputed control in order to count for victory.

SPECIAL RULES:   
**[19.23] Setting up the Stones of Power:**   
Before the start of the game, one Player takes nine Color Conversion Markers in each of the three colors (a total of 27 Blue, Red and Yellow Markers), and lays them flat in a mixed order on the table. The second Player now places a vortex marker on each of the Color Conversion Markers. The third Player then mixes these stacks of two markers thoroughly so that no one knows which vortex conceals which color. The first Player now places three of these two-marker combinations in each Gray city except Amma-Avattar. The Color Conversion Markers represent the Stones; the vortexes represent the Seals of Chaos.

**[19.24] Secret Deployment of Human Infantry:**   
If the Player desires, he may deploy any number of his available human infantry in any of the Gray cities (except Amma-Avattar). No more than two infantry units per city. These units are maintained off the map, and their locations (by hex number) recorded on a piece of paper. Hidden units may not move. They may come out of hiding (placed on the map) at the start of any Joint Combat Phase or at the start of any Owning-Player's Movement and Conjuration Phase. They may not be re-hidden. As long as they remain hidden, they are for all purposes not in play. Human infantry revealed during a Joint Combat Phase attack at doubled Strength in that Phase (defend normally).

**[19.25] Removing the Seals of Chaos and Revealingthe Stones of Power:**  
In order to remove a Seal from a Stone, the Sorcerer must be in the Gray city hex; he must be unpinned (though he may be depleted); he must announce his intention to remove the Seal and the number of Movement Points that he is expending to break the Seal. This may only be done during the Sorcerer’s Movement and Conjuration Phase. The presence of Enemy units in the same city hex has no effect upon this action. Seals are removed singly, although more than one Seal can be removed by the same Sorcerer during the same Movement and Conjuration
Phase. The above conditions must be met for each Seal removed. Each time a Sorcerer removes a Seal he is exposed to an immediate attack by that Seal. If the Sorcerer spends zero Movement Points to remove the Seal, the attack is read on the “0” Differential Column of the “Best” line of the Combat Results Table. For every Movement Point expended in breaking the Seal, the differential is reduced by one point. If, therefore, the Sorcerer spent eight Movement Points to remove a Seal, the attack would be read on the “-8” column and would have no effect upon the Sorcerer. Unlike other combat, the attack is unrelated to the Combat Strength of the Sorcerer or any of the units with him. The results of the attack affect only that Sorcerer and are applied immediately. Sorcerers may suffer a two-step loss in the same Movement and Conjuration Phase (and be eliminated) when breaking a Seal or series of Seals. Regardless of the effect on the Sorcerer, the Seal is 
always removed, and the Stone freed, whenever a Sorcerer announces he is breaking the Seal.  

It is always the topmost Seal and Stone combination in a city that is subject to removal and revelation. When a Seal is broken, remove the vortex marker from the map and take the exposed Color Conversion Marker off the remaining stack of Seals and Stones, leaving it in the city hex.

**[19.26] Moving and Controlling the Stones of Power:**   
Once a Stone is revealed (freed) it may be moved by any Sorcerer or human infantry unit
(magical units may move Stones of their own color). When a unit is carrying a Stone, it expends one Movement Point for each Stone carried for every Movement Point it expends in actual movement. If a Sorcerer carrying a Stone moved three hexes he would expend six Movement Points; if he were carrying three Stones, he would expend twelve Movement Points to travel the same distance. Any number of Stones of any colorfs) may be placed in the same hex. A Player has possession (control) of a Stone only when it is under one of his units (of any type). Players may steal Stones from one another by eliminating the owner’s units possessing the Stone and replacing them with his own units. Magical units may not move Stones into or out of Gray hexes.

**[19.27] Limitations on Sorcerers:**   
Sorcerers may not use teleportation. They may not engage each other in direct combat. They may throw bolts at each other and each other's units. They may attack Sorcerer units with magical and human infantry so long as they are not actually present in the hex in which the attack on a brother is made. Vortexes may not be created.

**[19.28] Ending the Game:**   
The game ends as soon as one Player has placed six Stones of his power color in a hex of the same color within two hexes of Amma-Avattar. The Stones must be under his control and his Sorcerer must be present in the hex. If this is not accomplished by the end of the **Tenth** Game-Turn, each of the surviving Players rolls the die to see if Ussa has given the charms of his power to Shamma, thereby preventing any of the remaining active Players from winning. If **all** of the surviving Players get a die roll **greater** than the number of Stones of their color under their control, then the game ends. For example, if three Players are still in the game and each controls five Stones of their respective color, each would have to roll a “6” in order for the game to end without a victory. This procedure is followed at the end of every Game-Turn from the Tenth onward.

**[19.29]** The Scenario may be played by four, five or six Players by adding a Green, an Orange, and/or a Purple Sorcerer with their human infantry complements. These odd color Sorcerers use the Stones over which they have direct dominance in color for victory purposes (e.g., a Green Sorcerer seeks the Yellow Stones). Their magical units may carry the Stones they seek.

#### [19.3] FANE OF KENNFANE AND THE PRIDE OF ISYA AND PAIRN
In the tenth Cycle of the Third Age, the Citylords of Demma, Ivor-Vorranna, and Riddella-Bes appealed to the newly-created League of Sorcerers for relief from the depredations of Fane, the White Sorcerer. Fane (who had gone mad struggling to control his six powers) had been creating enormous, very obscene apparitions in the public squares and in the skies over the three cities. Fane answered all reasonable appeals with a haughty giggle and the well-known pinky-finger-in-the-ear gesture. Isya and Pairn simultaneously volunteered to humiliate Fane by converting some of his White Realms into their own colors. Not exactly the best of friends, Isya and Pairn each insisted that the other was unequal to deal with Fane (who, though mad, was quite powerful). They went their separate
ways to challenge Fane and gave only lip service to the oath of cooperation that the League exacted from them. Only Fane's erratic behavior saved the two pompous Sorcerers from utter destruction. They did, however, convert the Realms (almost in spite of themselves) and so embarassed Fane that he spent the rest of his days giving puppet shows to his court. Obscene puppet shows.

**[18.31]** STARTING FORCES   
**White Player** (Fane)  
One White Sorcerer (Fane)   
Four human infantry   
One White (16) fortress in Fennfane (1112)  
Deployment: Sorcerer in fortress; human infantry in White cities.  

**Blue-Green-Yellow Player** (Pairn)  
One Blue-Green-Yellow Sorcerer (Pairn)  
One Green Sorcerer  
Four human infantry
Deployment: within three hexes of Riddella-Bes (1710)  

**Orange-Red-Purple Player** (Isya)  
One Orange-Red-Purple Sorcerer (Isya)  
One Red Sorcerer  
Four human infantry  
Deployment: within three hexes of Ivor-Vorranna (1016)  

GENERAL DEPLOYMENT:  
White deploys first, Blue-Green-Yellow second, Orange-Red-Purple last. Only White may initially deploy in White hexes. Blue-Green-Yellow moves first; White last.

**[19.32]** VICTORY CONDITIONS  
The non-White Players win if they convert more than half of the White hexes. If the non-White Players win. the one who has converted the most White hexes is the primary winner. The White Player wins by avoiding the non-White victory condition. Victory is evaluated at the end of ten Game-Turns.  

SPECIAL RULES:  
**[19.33] Fane’s Madness:**  
At the beginning of each Joint Combat Phase and each White Movement and Conjuration Phase, the White Player rolls the die and reads the color corresponding to that number in the center of the ColorValue Chart. This is the color that the White Sorcerer, his fortress and units will effectively be during that Phase. When engaging in combat with Enemy units of the same effective color, the attack is resolved on the “Neutral” line. During Enemy Movement and Conjuration Phases, the White Player’s units are pure White.

**1934] The Aegis of Chaos:**   
The White human infantry units are immune to the effects of vortexes at all times, whether or not their Sorcerer is with them. Fane himself is always immune to the effects of vortexes, regardless of the color he’s using.
